Australian Seller Guide

How to make a Pokémon card collection easier to assess.

A useful collection enquiry does not need to be perfect. It should make the important parts visible, show the condition clearly and explain enough context for a first review.

Start with the strongest items

Highlight vintage holos, modern chase cards, popular characters, graded slabs, sealed products and anything you already believe is valuable.

Separate cards by type

Group raw singles, slabs, sealed products, binders and bulk separately where possible. This makes the collection easier to understand at a glance.

Show condition honestly

Whitening, scratches, dents, bends, surface wear, seal issues and box damage are all useful to mention before any offer pathway.

Use a viewable photo link

A shared Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud, Imgur or spreadsheet link works best when permissions are set so Vaulture can view it without requesting access.

Include rough quantities

Approximate counts are enough for a first pass: total cards, binder pages, slab count, sealed items and bulk boxes all help.

Do not worry if you are unsure

Many sellers do not know exact values. Clear photos and a short description are usually a better starting point than waiting to identify every card.

Seller FAQ

Questions people ask before selling Pokémon cards.

The short version: send clear information, keep the link viewable and do not stress about making the enquiry perfect.

Does Vaulture buy Pokémon card collections in Australia?

Yes. Vaulture reviews collection enquiries from sellers across Australia, including singles, binders, graded cards, sealed products, bulk and larger holdings.

Do you buy graded Pokémon cards?

Yes. Include the grading company, grade, cert information if available and clear photos of the slab.

Do you buy sealed Pokémon TCG products?

Yes. Sealed product enquiries should include photos and notes about condition, seals, dents, tears or other packaging issues.

Can I sell a binder or childhood collection?

Yes. Binder and childhood collection enquiries are welcome. Approximate quantities, notable cards and condition notes help the review process.

Do I have to accept an offer?

No. Submitting the form does not commit you to selling. Any offer is subject to authenticity, condition and inspection.

How should I send photos?

Paste a shared link from Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud, Imgur, a spreadsheet or an online collection tracker. Make sure the link is viewable.

Can I sell Pokémon cards without knowing their value?

Yes. You can still submit an enquiry. Clear photos, rough quantities, notable cards and condition notes are enough to start a first review.

What affects the value of a Pokémon card collection?

Condition, rarity, demand, authenticity, grading, sealed product condition, set completeness and the amount of sorting required can all affect how a collection is reviewed.

Do you review bulk Pokémon cards?

Yes. Bulk and mixed lots can be included. Approximate counts, sorted categories and photos of the storage boxes or groups help the review process.
